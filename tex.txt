\section{(U) Introduction and Problem Statement}
%==========================================================================

\U Consider a dual-pulse solid rocket motor (SRM) in which two independent propellant grains (a \emph{sustain} grain and a \emph{second pulse} grain) share a common combustion chamber and exhaust through a single nozzle. Under nominal operation, the second pulse is ignited after the sustain grain has fully burned out; however, the present scenario of interest is when the second pulse is ignited \emph{during} the sustain burn, creating a period of simultaneous (overlapping) combustion.

\U The performance data for each grain (chamber pressure, mass flow rate, \& burn rate as functions of the percent web consumed) are available independently from each other, i.e., these datasets are not time-synchronized. When both grains burn simultaneously, the shared chamber pressure rises above the isolated value of either grain, which in turn modifies both burn rates, mass flow contributions, and burn durations of both grains. Therefore, a simple superposition of the isolated data is physically incorrect.

\U The objective of the present effort is to:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \U Correct the isolated grain data for the coupled chamber pressure during the overlapping burn period.
    \item \U Maintain proper time synchronization between the two grains on their independently discretized grids.
    \item \U Iteratively converge to a self-consistent solution for $\Pc(t)$, $\dot{m}(t)$, and the time history of each grain's fractional burn.
\end{enumerate}

\section{(U) Governing Physics}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{(U) Burn Rate Law}

\U The surface regression rate of a solid propellant follows Saint-Robert's law:
\begin{equation}
    \rdot_k = a_k(\Pc) \, \Pc^{\,n_k(\Pc)}
    \label{eq:burn_rate}
\end{equation}
where the subscript $k$ identifies the grain. In the general case, the burn rate coefficients $a_k$ and $n_k$ may vary with chamber pressure (i.e., different empirical values apply in different pressure regimes). In the simplest case, $a_k$ and $n_k$ are constants.

\U The implementation supports both cases via a piecewise burn rate table. Each grain carries a table of $[P_{c,\text{lower}},\; a,\; n]$ rows sorted by ascending pressure threshold. At a given $\Pc$, the row with the largest lower bound $\leq \Pc$ is selected:

\begin{lstlisting}
% Example: sustain grain with 3 pressure regimes
sus.br_table = [
    0,    3.20e-5, 0.35;   % Pc < 5 MPa
    5e6,  2.80e-5, 0.38;   % 5 MPa <= Pc < 10 MPa
    10e6, 2.50e-5, 0.40;   % Pc >= 10 MPa
];

% Example: pulse 2 with single a, n (constant)
p2.br_table = [0, 2.50e-5, 0.38];
\end{lstlisting}

\U Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_vs_burnrate} illustrates the burning rates of propellants as a function of pressure and initial temperature in a log-log scale. The pressure exponent $n_k$ is nearly independent of propellant temperature \cite{hill_peterson}, and is determined by the slope of the curves in Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_vs_burnrate}. The coefficient $a_k$ is a function of initial propellant temperature, but not a function of pressure; it is determined via the y-intercept of Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_vs_burnrate}. When the slope changes with pressure, multiple $(a, n)$ pairs are needed to characterize the propellant across its operating range.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{figures/pressure_vs_burnrate.png}
    \caption{(U) Burning rate characteristics of RDX composite propellants. (Courtesy Hill \& Peterson \cite{hill_peterson})}
    \label{fig:pressure_vs_burnrate}
\end{figure}

\subsection{(U) Mass Flow from a Grain}

\U The mass flow rate generated by grain $k$ at a given percent burn $\pctburn$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{m}_{g,k} =  \dot{r}_k \; \rho_k \; S_{burn,k}(\beta) =  a_k(\Pc) \; P_c^{n_k(\Pc)} \; \rho_k \; S_{burn,k}(\beta)
    \label{eq:mdot_grain}
\end{equation}
At a fixed $\beta$, the burn surface area $S_{burn,k}$ is constant. Therefore, the mass flow rate varies with chamber pressure only through the burn rate term.

\subsection{(U) Mass Flow Through the Nozzle}

\U The mass flow rate through the nozzle is:
\begin{equation}
    \mdotn = \frac{\Pc \; \At(\pctburn)}{\Cstar}
    \label{eq:mdot_nozzle}
\end{equation}

\U In the general case, the throat area $\At$ may vary with $\pctburn$ (e.g., due to nozzle erosion over the course of the burn). When $\At$ is constant, Eq.~\eqref{eq:mdot_nozzle} reduces to the standard form. The implementation supports both: each grain may carry an $\At(\pctburn)$ profile, or a constant scalar may be used.

\subsection{(U) Steady-State Equilibrium}

\U The quasi-steady assumption requires that mass generated by all actively burning grains equals mass exhausted through the nozzle at every instant:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{k \in \,\text{active}} \dot{m}_{g,k} = \mdotn
    \label{eq:mass_balance}
\end{equation}

\subsection{(U) Isolated Grain Equilibrium}

\U When a single grain burns alone with constant $a_k$ and $n_k$, combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mdot_grain} and \eqref{eq:mdot_nozzle} yields:
\begin{equation}
    S_{burn,k} \; \rho_k \; a_k \; \Pc^{\,n_k} = \frac{\Pc \; \At}{\Cstar}
\end{equation}

\U Which can be solved in closed form for $\Pc$:
\begin{equation}
    P_{c,\text{iso},k}(\beta) = \left( \frac{\rho_k \; S_{burn,k}(\beta) \; a_k \; \Cstar}{\At} \right)^{\!\frac{1}{1 - n_k}}
    \label{eq:Pc_iso}
\end{equation}

\U When $a_k$ and $n_k$ are pressure-dependent, or when $\At$ varies with $\pctburn$, the closed-form solution no longer applies. In this case, the isolated equilibrium is found by solving the implicit equation:
\begin{equation}
    S_{burn,k}(\pctburn) \; \rho_k \; a_k(\Pc) \; \Pc^{\,n_k(\Pc)} = \frac{\Pc \; \At(\pctburn)}{\Cstar}
    \label{eq:Pc_iso_implicit}
\end{equation}
at each $\pctburn$ using a bracketed root-finder.

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Mass Flow Correction for Change in Chamber Pressure}
\label{sec:mdot_correction}
%==========================================================================

\U When the burn rate parameters $a_k$ and $n_k$ are constant, a useful simplification exists. At a fixed fractional burn $\pctburn$, all quantities except $\Pc$ are constant. Therefore, if the chamber pressure changes from an isolated value $P_{c,\text{iso}}$ to some new value $P_{c,\text{new}}$, the mass flow rate scales as:

\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dot{m}_{g,\text{new}}}{\dot{m}_{g,\text{iso}}} = \frac{a_k \, P_{c,\text{new}}^{\,n_k}}{a_k \, P_{c,\text{iso}}^{\,n_k}} = \left( \frac{P_{c,\text{new}}}{P_{c,\text{iso}}} \right)^{n_k}
    \label{eq:mdot_correction}
\end{equation}

\U This relation allows the isolated grain data to be corrected to any chamber pressure without re-deriving the geometry or propellant properties.

\U When the burn rate parameters are pressure-dependent, this ratio shortcut does not hold because $a$ and $n$ themselves change with $\Pc$. In this case, the mass flow must be computed from the fundamental form (Eq.~\ref{eq:mdot_grain}) by evaluating $\rdot_k(\Pc)$ directly at the trial pressure using the appropriate $(a, n)$ regime from the burn rate table.

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Coupled Chamber Pressure During Overlap}
\label{sec:coupled_Pc}
%==========================================================================

\U When both grains burn simultaneously, the mass balance (Eq.~\ref{eq:mass_balance}) becomes:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{m}_{g,\text{sus}}(\Pc) + \dot{m}_{g,\text{2nd}}(\Pc) = \frac{\Pc \; \At(\pctburn)}{\Cstar}
    \label{eq:coupled_balance}
\end{equation}

\U In the general form (supporting pressure-dependent burn rate parameters and variable throat area), this is:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    S_{burn,\text{sus}} \, \rho_{\text{sus}} \, a_{\text{sus}}(\Pc) \, \Pc^{\,n_{\text{sus}}(\Pc)}
    \;+\;
    S_{burn,\text{2nd}} \, \rho_{\text{2nd}} \, a_{\text{2nd}}(\Pc) \, \Pc^{\,n_{\text{2nd}}(\Pc)}
    \;=\;
    \frac{\Pc \; \At(\pctburn)}{\Cstar}
    }
    \label{eq:Pc_implicit}
\end{equation}

\U This is an implicit, nonlinear equation in the single unknown $\Pc$. All other quantities are known from the grain data at the current $\pctburn$ of each grain. When $a$ and $n$ are constant, this simplifies to the ratio-based form shown in Section~\ref{sec:mdot_correction}.

\subsection{(U) Root Structure}

\U The left-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pc_implicit} grows as $\Pc^{\,n}$ with $n < 1$, while the right-hand side grows linearly with $\Pc$. At low pressures the mass generation dominates; at high pressures the nozzle flow dominates. This guarantees exactly one positive root, which can be found reliably by a bracketed root-finder (e.g., Brent's method via \texttt{fzero} in MATLAB) on the interval $[P_{c,\text{low}}, \, P_{c,\text{hi}}]$ with $P_{c,\text{low}} > 0$.

\subsection{(U) Note on Characteristic Velocity}

\U Equation~\eqref{eq:Pc_implicit} uses a single $\Cstar$ for the nozzle flow. If the two propellants have significantly different thermochemical properties, a mass-weighted effective characteristic velocity should be used:
\begin{equation}
    C^*_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\dot{m}_{g,\text{sus}} \, C^*_{\text{sus}} + \dot{m}_{g,\text{2nd}} \, C^*_{\text{2nd}}}{\dot{m}_{g,\text{sus}} + \dot{m}_{g,\text{2nd}}}
\end{equation}

\U In this demonstration, the propellants are assumed to be similar enough to where a single $\Cstar$ is acceptable.

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Time Vector Construction}
\label{sec:time_construction}
%==========================================================================

\U The fractional burn $\pctburn$ represents the fraction of total web thickness consumed. The physical web depth regressed between two adjacent discretization points is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta w_i = w \cdot (\pctburn_{i+1} - \pctburn_i) = w \cdot \Delta\pctburn_i
    \label{eq:dw}
\end{equation}

\U The time required to regress through this depth is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i = \frac{\Delta w_i}{\bar{\rdot}_i}
    \label{eq:dt}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\rdot}_i$ is the trapezoidal average of the burn rate over the interval:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\rdot}_i = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\rdot(\pctburn_i) + \rdot(\pctburn_{i+1})\bigr)
    \label{eq:rdot_avg}
\end{equation}

\U The cumulative time vector is then:
\begin{equation}
    t_j = \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} \Delta t_i, \qquad t_0 = 0
    \label{eq:t_cum}
\end{equation}

\subsection{(U) Justification: Why $\Delta w / \bar{\rdot}$ and Not $\Delta m / \bar{\dot{m}}$}
\label{sec:dt_justification}

\U An alternative formulation that is sometimes proposed computes the time increment from the mass consumed and the mass flow rate:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{(\text{mass})} = \frac{\Delta m_i}{\bar{\dot{m}}_i}
    \label{eq:dt_mass}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta m_i = \rho \, \bar{S}_{burn,i} \, \Delta w_i$ is the mass of propellant in the web slice and $\bar{\dot{m}}_i = \frac{1}{2}(\dot{m}_i + \dot{m}_{i+1})$. This section demonstrates that the web-based formulation (Eq.~\ref{eq:dt}) is the physically correct choice and that the mass-based alternative introduces error for non-neutral grain geometries.

\U \textbf{Equivalence for neutral grains.} Expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:dt_mass}:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{(\text{mass})} = \frac{\rho \, \bar{S}_{burn,i} \, \Delta w_i}{\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\rho \, S_{burn,i} \, \rdot_i \;+\; \rho \, S_{burn,i+1} \, \rdot_{i+1}\bigr)}
\end{equation}

\U When the burn surface area is constant across the interval ($S_{burn,i} = S_{burn,i+1} = \bar{S}_{burn,i}$), the $\rho S$ terms cancel identically:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{(\text{mass})} = \frac{\Delta w_i}{\frac{1}{2}(\rdot_i + \rdot_{i+1})} = \frac{\Delta w_i}{\bar{\rdot}_i} = \Delta t_i
\end{equation}

\U The two formulations are equivalent for a neutral grain (constant $\Sburn$).

\U \textbf{Discrepancy for non-neutral grains.} For progressive or regressive grain geometries where $S_{burn,i} \neq S_{burn,i+1}$, the mass-based formulation conflates two distinct physical processes: (1) the speed at which the burn surface advances into the propellant (the regression rate $\rdot$, in m/s), and (2) the rate at which propellant mass is consumed ($\dot{m}$, in kg/s).

\U The fractional burn $\pctburn$ tracks the \emph{position of the burn front} as a fraction of total web thickness. The time to advance the burn front from position $\pctburn_i$ to $\pctburn_{i+1}$ depends solely on the regression rate $\rdot$, which is a velocity (m/s) normal to the burn surface. The surface area $\Sburn$ determines how much mass is liberated per unit of web regressed, but it does not affect the speed at which the web is consumed.

\U To make this concrete, consider two grains with identical propellant ($\rho$, $a$, $n$), identical web thickness, and identical chamber pressure, but different geometries: one has $\Sburn = 0.1$~m$^2$ (end-burner) and the other has $\Sburn = 1.0$~m$^2$ (large cylindrical port). Both grains regress at the same rate $\rdot = a \Pc^n$ because the regression rate is a local propellant property and does not depend on the total exposed surface area. They therefore consume their web in the same amount of time, despite one producing 10$\times$ the mass flow. The web-based formulation correctly gives identical $\Delta t$ for both; the mass-based formulation would not.

\U \textbf{Summary.} Because $\pctburn$ is defined as fractional \emph{web} consumption (not fractional \emph{mass} consumption), the time increment must be derived from the web regression rate $\rdot$. The mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ is a derived quantity used for the chamber pressure equilibrium, but it is not the appropriate basis for the time vector.

\subsection{(U) Time Correction Under Changed Chamber Pressure}

\U When the chamber pressure changes from $P_{c,\text{old}}$ to $P_{c,\text{new}}$ (due to overlap coupling), the burn rate changes, and therefore $\Delta t$ must be corrected. The general correction uses the ratio of actual burn rates:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{\text{new}} = \Delta t_i^{\text{old}} \cdot \frac{\rdot(\bar{P}_{c,\text{old},i})}{\rdot(\bar{P}_{c,\text{new},i})}
    \label{eq:dt_correction}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\Pc}_i$ denotes the average of the pressure at the two endpoints of interval $i$. Higher pressure yields a faster burn rate and thus a shorter time increment.

\U When $a$ and $n$ are constant, this simplifies to:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{\text{new}} = \Delta t_i^{\text{old}} \cdot \left( \frac{\bar{P}_{c,\text{old},i}}{\bar{P}_{c,\text{new},i}} \right)^{n}
    \label{eq:dt_correction_simple}
\end{equation}

\U The implementation uses the general form (Eq.~\ref{eq:dt_correction}) via the \texttt{eval\_rdot} function, which correctly handles pressure-dependent $a$ and $n$:

\begin{lstlisting}
rdot_old = eval_rdot(Pc_old_avg, grain.br_table);
rdot_new = eval_rdot(Pc_new_avg, grain.br_table);
dt_new(j) = dt_iso(j) * rdot_old / rdot_new;
\end{lstlisting}

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Time Synchronization Between Grains}
\label{sec:time_sync}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{(U) The Core Challenge}

\U Each grain's isolated data is defined on its own independent fractional burn grid $\pctburn_k$ with its own number of discretization points $N_k$. These grids need not have the same spacing or number of points. The isolated time vectors $t_k(\pctburn)$ are also different. To evaluate the coupled equilibrium (Eq.~\ref{eq:Pc_implicit}) at any instant, we must know each grain's fractional burn at that instant, which requires mapping one grain's $\pctburn$ onto the other's time grid.

\subsection{(U) Primary and Secondary Grain Selection}
\label{sec:prim_sec}

\U The model shall designate one grain as the \emph{primary} (time reference) and the other as the \emph{secondary}. The primary grain's time vector serves as the master timeline; the secondary grain's fractional burn is interpolated onto this timeline.

\U \textbf{Selection rule:} The primary grain is whichever grain has the \emph{longer remaining burn duration} from the moment of second pulse ignition.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \U If the second pulse is ignited early in the sustain burn, and sustain has significant burn remaining that exceeds Pulse~2's total duration, then sustain is the primary.
    \item \U If the second pulse is ignited very late, and sustain's remaining duration is less than Pulse~2's total burn duration, then Pulse~2 is the primary.
\end{itemize}

\U This choice ensures that the master timeline never runs out before the secondary grain burns out. Once the secondary grain reaches $\pctburn = 1$ (burnout), its mass flow contribution drops to zero and the model naturally transitions to single-grain equilibrium on the primary grain's timeline.

\subsection{(U) Interpolation in Fractional-Burn Space}

\U When correcting the secondary grain's time increments under a new chamber pressure, the interpolation is performed in \emph{fractional-burn space} rather than time space. Specifically, the combined $\Pc$ is known at the primary grain's $\pctburn$ grid points, and the secondary grain's $\pctburn$ at those same primary time points is also known from the time-alignment step. This yields a mapping:
\begin{equation}
    \Pc \;\leftrightarrow\; \pctburn_{\text{sec}}
\end{equation}
which can be interpolated onto the secondary grain's native $\pctburn$ grid to obtain $\Pc(\pctburn_{\text{sec}})$. This is more robust than interpolating in time space because:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \U $\pctburn$ is bounded on $[0,1]$ and monotonically increasing.
    \item \U The time vectors shift between iterations (as $\Delta t$ is corrected), making time-based interpolation targets stale after each iteration.
\end{itemize}

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Three-Phase Timeline Structure}
\label{sec:phases}
%==========================================================================

\U The full motor timeline is divided into three distinct phases:

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
    \item[\U Phase 1: Sustain Alone ($0 \leq t < t_{\text{ign}}$)]
    \U Only the sustain grain is burning. The chamber pressure is the sustain grain's isolated equilibrium $P_{c,\text{sus,iso}}(t)$. No correction is needed.

    \item[\U Phase 2: Overlap ($t_{\text{ign}} \leq t \leq t_{\text{sec,burnout}}$)]
    \U Both grains burn simultaneously. The coupled equilibrium (Eq.~\ref{eq:Pc_implicit}) must be solved iteratively. The chamber pressure rises above either grain's isolated value. The secondary grain's burnout marks the end of this phase.

    \item[\U Phase 3: Trailing Grain Alone ($t_{\text{sec,burnout}} < t \leq t_{\text{prim,burnout}}$)]
    \U Only the primary grain remains. The model reduces to single-grain equilibrium. The boundary condition at entry is the chamber pressure at the moment of secondary burnout.
\end{description}

\U Note that Phases 2 and 3 are handled seamlessly by the solver: once the secondary grain's $\pctburn$ reaches 1, its mass flow is set to zero, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pc_implicit} naturally degenerates to a single-grain equation.

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Iterative Solution Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}
%==========================================================================

\U The algorithm is a marching-in-iterations scheme. Each iteration refines the chamber pressure, mass flow rates, and time vectors toward self-consistency.

\subsection{(U) Initialization: Load Isolated Grain Data}

\U Each grain is loaded as an independent data structure containing its native $\pctburn$ grid, burn rate table, optional throat area profile, and all associated isolated performance data. The two grains may have different numbers of discretization points and different grid spacings.

\begin{lstlisting}
[sus, p2] = load_grain_data(At_const, Cstar);
sus = normalize_grain(sus, At_const);
p2  = normalize_grain(p2,  At_const);
\end{lstlisting}

\U The \texttt{normalize\_grain} function converts scalar $a$/$n$ to a single-row \texttt{br\_table} and expands a missing or scalar \texttt{At} to a constant vector, so the solver only handles one format.

\U For grains with pressure-dependent burn rate parameters, the isolated equilibrium at each $\pctburn$ is found by solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:Pc_iso_implicit} numerically:

\begin{lstlisting}
f = @(Pc) Sb_i * rho_i * eval_rdot(Pc, brt) - Pc * At_i / Cstar;
grain.Pc_iso(i) = fzero(f, [1e3, Pc_hi], opts);
grain.rdot_iso(i) = eval_rdot(grain.Pc_iso(i), brt);
grain.mdot_iso(i) = grain.Pc_iso(i) * At_i / Cstar;
\end{lstlisting}

\U For grains with constant $a$ and $n$ and constant $\At$, the closed form (Eq.~\ref{eq:Pc_iso}) is used directly:

\begin{lstlisting}
K = grain.rho * grain.Sburn(i) * grain.a_br * Cstar / At;
grain.Pc_iso(i)   = K^(1 / (1 - grain.n_br));
grain.rdot_iso(i) = grain.a_br * grain.Pc_iso(i)^grain.n_br;
grain.mdot_iso(i) = grain.Pc_iso(i) * At / Cstar;
\end{lstlisting}

\U The time vector is constructed from the web regression increments (see Section~\ref{sec:time_construction} and \ref{sec:dt_justification}):

\begin{lstlisting}
dw       = grain.web * diff(grain.pct);
rdot_avg = 0.5 * (grain.rdot_iso(1:end-1) + grain.rdot_iso(2:end));
grain.dt    = dw ./ rdot_avg;
grain.t_cum = [0; cumsum(grain.dt)];
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Initialization: Determine Overlap Parameters}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U Given the ignition time $t_{\text{ign}}$, the sustain grain's fractional burn at ignition is found by interpolating from its time-vs-$\pctburn$ relationship:
\begin{equation}
    \pctburn_{\text{sus,ign}} = \text{interp}\bigl(t_{\text{sus}},\; \pctburn_{\text{sus}},\; t_{\text{ign}}\bigr)
\end{equation}

\begin{lstlisting}
pct_sus_at_ign = interp1(sus.t_cum, sus.pct, t_ignite_p2, 'linear');

sus_time_remaining = sus.t_cum(end) - t_ignite_p2;
p2_time_remaining  = p2.t_cum(end);
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Initialization: Extract Sustain Remainder}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U Only the portion of sustain's data from $\pctburn \geq \pctburn_{\text{sus,ign}}$ participates in the overlap. The sustain grain's native grid points at or above the ignition $\pctburn$ are retained. If $t_{\text{ign}}$ falls between grid points, an interpolated point is prepended at the exact ignition $\pctburn$ to avoid losing the boundary.

\begin{lstlisting}
sus_rem_mask = sus.pct >= pct_sus_at_ign;
sus_rem_pct  = sus.pct(sus_rem_mask);

% Prepend interpolated point if ignition falls between grid points
if sus_rem_pct(1) > pct_sus_at_ign + 1e-12
    sus_rem_pct = [pct_sus_at_ign; sus_rem_pct];
end

sus_rem.Pc_iso   = interp1(sus.pct, sus.Pc_iso,   sus_rem_pct, 'linear');
sus_rem.mdot_iso = interp1(sus.pct, sus.mdot_iso,  sus_rem_pct, 'linear');
sus_rem.rdot_iso = interp1(sus.pct, sus.rdot_iso,  sus_rem_pct, 'linear');
sus_rem.Sburn    = interp1(sus.pct, sus.Sburn,     sus_rem_pct, 'linear');
sus_rem.At       = interp1(sus.pct, sus.At,         sus_rem_pct, 'linear');
\end{lstlisting}

\U The time vector for the remaining sustain portion is recomputed from $t = 0$ (the moment of Pulse~2 ignition):

\begin{lstlisting}
dw_sus = sus.web * diff(sus_rem_pct);
rdot_avg_sus = 0.5 * (sus_rem.rdot_iso(1:end-1) + sus_rem.rdot_iso(2:end));
sus_rem.dt_iso = dw_sus ./ rdot_avg_sus;
sus_rem.t_cum  = [0; cumsum(sus_rem.dt_iso)];
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Initialization: Primary and Secondary Grain Selection}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U The grain with the longer remaining burn duration is designated as the \emph{primary} (time reference). The other becomes the \emph{secondary}, whose $\pctburn$ is interpolated onto the primary's timeline. This ensures the master time grid never runs out before the secondary burns out.

\begin{lstlisting}
if p2_time_remaining >= sus_time_remaining
    prim     = p2_rem;   prim_pct = p2_rem_pct;
    sec      = sus_rem;  sec_pct  = sus_rem_pct;
else
    prim     = sus_rem;  prim_pct = sus_rem_pct;
    sec      = p2_rem;   sec_pct  = p2_rem_pct;
end
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Initialization: Allocate Working Storage}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U Working copies of the time vectors are created for both grains. These are updated at each iteration as the chamber pressure correction changes the burn rates and thus the time increments.

\begin{lstlisting}
N_prim = length(prim_pct);

prim_dt = prim.dt_iso;
prim_t  = prim.t_cum;
sec_dt  = sec.dt_iso;
sec_t   = sec.t_cum;

Pc_combined    = zeros(N_prim, 1);
Pc_prev        = zeros(N_prim, 1);
mdot_prim_corr = zeros(N_prim, 1);
mdot_sec_corr  = zeros(N_prim, 1);
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Iteration Step 1: Time-Align Fractional Burns}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U At each primary grid point $j$, the secondary grain's fractional burn is determined by interpolating the secondary's $\pctburn$-vs-time curve at the primary's current time $t_j^{\text{prim}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \pctburn_{\text{sec}}(t_j^{\text{prim}}) = \text{interp}\bigl(t_{\text{sec}},\; \pctburn_{\text{sec}},\; t_j^{\text{prim}}\bigr)
\end{equation}

\U If the primary time exceeds the secondary's total burn time, the secondary is marked as inactive (burned out, $\pctburn = 1$) and its mass flow contribution is zeroed.

\begin{lstlisting}
sec_pct_on_prim = zeros(N_prim, 1);
sec_active      = true(N_prim, 1);

for j = 1:N_prim
    tj = prim_t(j);
    if tj <= sec_t(end)
        sec_pct_on_prim(j) = interp1(sec_t, sec_pct, tj, 'linear');
    else
        sec_pct_on_prim(j) = sec_pct(end);  % burned out
        sec_active(j) = false;
    end
end
\end{lstlisting}

\U The secondary grain's isolated $\Sburn$ and $\Pc$ at the mapped $\pctburn$ values are looked up by interpolating from the secondary's native grid:

\begin{lstlisting}
sec_Sburn_mapped = interp1(sec_pct, sec.Sburn, sec_pct_on_prim, 'linear', 'extrap');
sec_Pc_mapped    = interp1(sec_pct, sec.Pc_iso, sec_pct_on_prim, 'linear', 'extrap');

sec_Sburn_mapped(~sec_active) = 0;
sec_Pc_mapped(~sec_active)    = 0;
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Iteration Step 2: Solve Coupled Chamber Pressure}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U At each primary grid point $j$, the coupled steady-state equilibrium is solved using the fundamental mass balance (Eq.~\ref{eq:Pc_implicit}). The residual function evaluates the burn rate directly from the burn rate table at each trial $\Pc$, correctly handling pressure-dependent $a$ and $n$:
\begin{equation}
    f(\Pc) = S_{burn,p} \, \rho_p \, \rdot_p(\Pc)
    \;+\; S_{burn,s} \, \rho_s \, \rdot_s(\Pc)
    \;-\; \frac{\Pc \; \At(\pctburn)}{\Cstar}
    = 0
\end{equation}

\U The throat area $\At$ at each $\pctburn$ is taken from the primary grain's profile.

\begin{lstlisting}
for j = 1:N_prim
    Sb_p  = prim.Sburn(j);
    rho_p = prim.rho;
    At_j  = prim.At(j);

    if sec_active(j)
        Sb_s  = sec_Sburn_mapped(j);
        rho_s = sec.rho;

        f = @(Pc) Sb_p * rho_p * eval_rdot(Pc, prim.br_table) ...
                + Sb_s * rho_s * eval_rdot(Pc, sec.br_table) ...
                - Pc * At_j / Cstar;
    else
        f = @(Pc) Sb_p * rho_p * eval_rdot(Pc, prim.br_table) ...
                - Pc * At_j / Cstar;
    end

    Pc_lo = 1e3;
    Pc_hi = max(Pc0 * 5, 100e6);
    Pc_combined(j) = fzero(f, [Pc_lo, Pc_hi], opts);
\end{lstlisting}

\U The corrected mass flow rates for each grain are stored:
\begin{align}
    \dot{m}_{\text{prim}}^{(m)}(j) &= S_{burn,p}(j) \; \rho_p \; \rdot_p\bigl(\Pc^{(m)}(j)\bigr) \\[4pt]
    \dot{m}_{\text{sec}}^{(m)}(j) &= S_{burn,s}(j) \; \rho_s \; \rdot_s\bigl(\Pc^{(m)}(j)\bigr)
\end{align}

\begin{lstlisting}
    mdot_prim_corr(j) = Sb_p * rho_p * eval_rdot(Pc_combined(j), prim.br_table);
    if sec_active(j)
        mdot_sec_corr(j) = Sb_s * rho_s * eval_rdot(Pc_combined(j), sec.br_table);
    else
        mdot_sec_corr(j) = 0;
    end
end
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Iteration Step 3: Check Convergence}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U The maximum relative change in chamber pressure across all grid points is computed:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon^{(m)} = \max_j \left| \frac{\Pc^{(m)}(j) - \Pc^{(m-1)}(j)}{\Pc^{(m-1)}(j)} \right|
\end{equation}

\U If $\epsilon^{(m)} < \text{tol}$, the solution is converged and the iteration loop terminates.

\begin{lstlisting}
if iter > 1
    valid = Pc_prev > 0;
    rel_change = max(abs(Pc_combined(valid) - Pc_prev(valid)) ./ Pc_prev(valid));
    if rel_change < tol
        converged = true;
        break;
    end
end
Pc_prev = Pc_combined;
\end{lstlisting}

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Iteration Step 4: Correct Time Vectors}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U Under the new combined $\Pc$, each grain's burn rate changes, which modifies the time required to regress through each web increment. Using the general burn rate ratio (Eq.~\ref{eq:dt_correction}):
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t_i^{(m)} = \Delta t_i^{\text{iso}} \cdot \frac{\rdot(\bar{P}_{c,\text{iso},i})}{\rdot(\bar{P}_{c,i}^{(m)})}
\end{equation}

\U This correctly handles pressure-dependent $a$ and $n$ because the burn rate is evaluated directly from the table at each pressure, rather than relying on a simple power-law ratio.

\subsubsection{(U) Primary Grain}

\U The combined $\Pc^{(m)}$ is already defined on the primary grain's $\pctburn$ grid, so the correction is applied directly:

\begin{lstlisting}
for j = 1:length(prim_dt)
    Pc_old_avg = 0.5 * (prim.Pc_iso(j) + prim.Pc_iso(j+1));
    Pc_new_avg = 0.5 * (Pc_combined(j) + Pc_combined(j+1));
    if isfinite(Pc_new_avg) && Pc_new_avg > 0
        rdot_old = eval_rdot(Pc_old_avg, prim.br_table);
        rdot_new = eval_rdot(Pc_new_avg, prim.br_table);
        prim_dt(j) = prim.dt_iso(j) * rdot_old / rdot_new;
    end
end
prim_t = [0; cumsum(prim_dt)];
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{(U) Secondary Grain}

\U The combined $\Pc^{(m)}$ is not directly defined on the secondary grain's native $\pctburn$ grid. However, from Step~1 we know the secondary's $\pctburn$ at each primary grid point where the secondary was active. This provides a mapping $\Pc^{(m)} \leftrightarrow \pctburn_{\text{sec}}$, which is interpolated onto the secondary's native grid in \emph{fractional-burn space}:
\begin{equation}
    \Pc^{(m)}(\pctburn_{\text{sec},i}) = \text{interp}\bigl(\pctburn_{\text{sec,mapped}},\; \Pc^{(m)},\; \pctburn_{\text{sec},i}\bigr)
\end{equation}

\U This is more robust than interpolating in time space because $\pctburn$ is bounded on $[0,1]$ and monotonically increasing, whereas the time vectors shift between iterations.

\begin{lstlisting}
active_idx = find(sec_active);
if ~isempty(active_idx)
    sec_pct_mapped = sec_pct_on_prim(active_idx);
    Pc_mapped      = Pc_combined(active_idx);

    [sec_pct_mapped, uniq_idx] = unique(sec_pct_mapped);
    Pc_mapped = Pc_mapped(uniq_idx);

    if length(sec_pct_mapped) >= 2
        Pc_at_sec_pct = interp1(sec_pct_mapped, Pc_mapped, sec_pct, ...
                                'linear', 'extrap');
        Pc_at_sec_pct = max(Pc_at_sec_pct, 1e3);

        for j = 1:length(sec_dt)
            Pc_old_avg = 0.5 * (sec.Pc_iso(j) + sec.Pc_iso(j+1));
            Pc_new_avg = 0.5 * (Pc_at_sec_pct(j) + Pc_at_sec_pct(j+1));
            rdot_old = eval_rdot(Pc_old_avg, sec.br_table);
            rdot_new = eval_rdot(Pc_new_avg, sec.br_table);
            sec_dt(j) = sec.dt_iso(j) * rdot_old / rdot_new;
        end
    end
end
sec_t = [0; cumsum(sec_dt)];
\end{lstlisting}

\U Both cumulative time vectors are rebuilt from the corrected $\Delta t$ values, and the iteration returns to Step~1 for the next pass.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{(U) Post-Convergence: Assemble Full Timeline}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------------

\U After the iteration converges, the full motor timeline is assembled from three phases. Phase~1 (sustain alone) uses the original isolated sustain data up to the ignition time. Phases~2 and~3 (overlap and trailing grain) come directly from the converged solver output, which already handles the transition naturally---once the secondary grain's $\pctburn$ reaches 1, its mass flow is zero and $\Pc$ reduces to single-grain equilibrium.

\begin{lstlisting}
% Phase 1: Sustain alone (motor start to Pulse 2 ignition)
phase1_mask = sus.t_cum < t_ignite_p2;
phase1_t    = sus.t_cum(phase1_mask);
phase1_Pc   = sus.Pc_iso(phase1_mask);

% Phases 2+3: Overlap + trailing grain
overlap_t_global = prim_t + t_ignite_p2;

% Combine (avoid duplicate at junction)
full_t  = [phase1_t;  overlap_t_global(2:end)];
full_Pc = [phase1_Pc; Pc_combined(2:end)];

% Total mass flow from steady state (using variable At)
full_mdot = full_Pc .* full_At / Cstar;
\end{lstlisting}

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Summary of Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}
%==========================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \U \textbf{Quasi-steady state.} At every instant, mass generated equals mass exhausted. Chamber filling/emptying transients are neglected. This is valid when the chamber volume is small relative to the mass flow rate (acoustic timescale $\ll$ burn timescale).

    \item \U \textbf{Saint-Robert's burn rate law.} The regression rate follows $\rdot = a(\Pc) \, \Pc^{n(\Pc)}$, where $a$ and $n$ may be piecewise-constant functions of chamber pressure. Erosive burning, pressure-coupled instabilities, and temperature sensitivity are neglected.

    \item \U \textbf{Choked nozzle flow.} The nozzle remains choked throughout the burn, so $\mdotn = \Pc \, \At(\pctburn) / \Cstar$ holds at all times. The throat area may vary with $\pctburn$ (e.g., nozzle erosion).

    \item \U \textbf{Single characteristic velocity.} A single $\Cstar$ is used for the nozzle mass flow, implying both propellants produce combustion products with similar thermochemical properties. For dissimilar propellants, a mass-weighted $\Cstar_{\text{eff}}$ should be substituted.

    \item \U \textbf{Burn surface area depends only on fractional burn.} $\Sburn(\pctburn)$ is a known function for each grain, independent of pressure or time.

    \item \U \textbf{Instantaneous ignition.} The second pulse begins contributing mass flow immediately at $t_{\text{ign}}$ with no ignition transient or delay.

    \item \U \textbf{Instantaneous pressure equilibration.} When a grain ignites or burns out, the chamber pressure adjusts immediately to the new equilibrium. In reality, there would be a finite pressurization/depressurization transient.

    \item \U \textbf{No thermal or mechanical coupling between grains.} The elevated chamber temperature during overlap does not affect the opposing grain's propellant properties (e.g., no cook-off or accelerated decomposition effects).

    \item \U \textbf{Independent grain data grids.} Each grain's isolated data may have a different number of discretization points and grid spacing. All cross-grain operations use linear interpolation.
\end{enumerate}

%==========================================================================
\section{(U) Nonlinear Pressure Amplification}
\label{sec:nonlinear}
%==========================================================================

\U An important physical result is that the combined chamber pressure during overlap is \emph{significantly higher} than the sum of the two isolated pressures. This is a direct consequence of the nonlinearity in the equilibrium equation.

\U For a single grain, $P_{c,\text{iso}} \propto (\rho \, \Sburn \, a)^{1/(1-n)}$. When a second grain is added, the effective $\rho \, \Sburn \, a$ product increases, and the $1/(1-n)$ exponent amplifies this increase. For typical values of $n \approx 0.35$:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{1}{1 - n} \approx 1.54
\end{equation}
so even a modest increase in total mass generation produces a disproportionately large pressure rise. This is why the naive estimate of $P_{c,\text{total}} \approx P_{c,\text{sus}} + P_{c,\text{2nd}}$ consistently \emph{underestimates} the true coupled pressure, and the iterative correction converges to a substantially higher value.
